G.2 The elite boutiques, firm by firm

Public-record sketches: what each house is known for, its marquee franchises, and how to aim your preparation. Verify current group structures on the firms' own sites: teams move.

M&A + RX + activism defense

Evercore

The largest of the independents by revenue: a full advisory platform with top-tier M&A, a serious restructuring group, equities research, and a strong activism-defense practice. Senior bankers largely ex-bulge-bracket rainmakers; known for putting analysts on lean teams.

Prep aim: M&A depth (merger models, defense), plus 2 recent Evercore-advised deals discussed intelligently. RX candidates: full waterfall fluency.
RX royalty + strategic advisory

PJT Partners

Spun from Blackstone's advisory arm in 2015. RSSG (Restructuring & Special Situations) is the crown jewel: top-ranked in major reorganizations (their own materials cite PG&E, WeWork, Revlon, Puerto Rico class mandates). Also Strategic Advisory (M&A), shareholder advisory, and PJT Park Hill (fund placement).

Prep aim: for RSSG: the full restructuring canon (the worked example was built on their posting archetype): distressed screening, 13-week cash flows, Chapter 11 mechanics, leveraged-credit market map.
The original independent

Lazard

The 175-year-old house: global M&A advisory with particular strength in Europe and cross-border, a long-standing restructuring franchise (sovereigns included: it advises countries), and an asset-management arm that makes the listed company a hybrid.

Prep aim: cross-border deal mechanics and a view on European M&A; RX candidates should know its sovereign-debt work exists and differs from corporate RX.
Fewest, largest, quietest

Centerview Partners

The high-fee-per-deal model at its purest: a partnership famous for mega-cap board relationships, healthcare and consumer strength, and for staying private and press-shy. Known for paying analysts at or above the top of the street and hiring sparingly.

Prep aim: board-level judgment questions: why deals happen, activism dynamics, and 2 or 3 of its marquee mandates from public reports. Expect heavy fit screening.
Founder-led dealmaking

Moelis & Company

Ken Moelis's 2007 boutique: entrepreneurial culture, strong sponsor relationships, an active RX practice, and deep Middle East franchise. Known for lean teams and early responsibility, with the intensity that implies.

Prep aim: sponsor-adjacent fluency (LBO math cold), plus comfort discussing restructuring basics: the practices cross-staff.
RX volume king (mid-cap)

Houlihan Lokey

The most active restructuring advisor by deal count cycle after cycle, dominant in middle-market M&A and creditor-side mandates, with big valuation and financial-advisory businesses besides. The seat that sees the most RX reps per year, full stop.

Prep aim: creditor-side perspective: committee dynamics, fulcrum analysis from the lender's chair, and mid-cap credit metrics. Volume culture: speed matters.
Partnership craft, advisory + capital solutions

Perella Weinberg / Rothschild & Co

PWP: senior-heavy advisory with respected RX and healthcare/energy franchises. Rothschild: the European powerhouse: enormous deal counts across Europe, global M&A plus debt advisory and equity markets advisory; the family-controlled model is its own study.

Prep aim: for Rothschild especially: European market structure and mid-cap cross-border flow; debt-advisory candidates need refinancing mechanics.
Tech M&A specialists

Qatalyst Partners

Frank Quattrone's firm: sell-side advisor of choice in large-cap tech, famous for extracting premium prices in take-privates and strategic sales. Tiny classes, extreme selectivity, deep technical-plus-sector interviews.

Prep aim: tech sector metrics as first language (ARR, NRR, rule of 40: the SaaS profile is the syllabus), plus every major tech deal of the past year.

Within big banks, the group defines the experience more than the logo. The recurring archetypes:

Choosing between archetypes. Optimize for reps in the product you want to master, seniors who will know your name, and deal flow you can verify (announced mandates are public: count them).
