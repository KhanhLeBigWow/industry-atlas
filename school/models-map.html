M.2 The models, seat by seat

Sell-side · advisory

M&A group

Models on your screen
Merger model (accretion/dilution with PPA and synergies), DCF, trading comps and precedents, ability-to-pay LBO (what a sponsor could bid), sum-of-the-parts, and the football field that stitches them together.
Drivers that matter
Price and premium, financing mix, synergies net of costs-to-achieve, purchase accounting (intangible amortization), pro forma leverage.
The output that decides
The accretion/dilution table across financing scenarios, and where the offer lands on the football field.
How it is tested
Merger math aloud, then judgment: is accretive good? Where did the synergies go? Module 05 + the Drill Room's snap calls.
Sell-side · advisory

Restructuring (RX)

Models on your screen
Capital structure waterfall (recovery by class at a range of EVs), 13-week cash flow, liquidity runway model, DIP sizing, and the reorganized-company business plan model that valuation fights are fought over.
Drivers that matter
Enterprise value (the whole fight), class-by-class claims and liens, cash burn by week, DIP terms, plan treatment.
The output that decides
Where value breaks: the fulcrum security, and each class's recovery per point of EV.
How it is tested
Build a waterfall from a cap table, find the fulcrum, defend the EV. The interview bank's RX category is the verbal version.
Buy-side · control

Private equity (buyout)

Models on your screen
The LBO model in every grade: paper, 3-hour test, full operating model with debt schedule and cash sweep; plus add-on models, covenant models, and the monthly portfolio-company reporting build.
Drivers that matter
Entry/exit multiple, leverage and its cost, EBITDA growth by lever (price, volume, cost, add-ons), FCF conversion, fees.
The output that decides
MOIC and IRR with attribution: how much came from operations vs leverage vs multiple.
How it is tested
Paper LBO aloud, timed model test, then the case-study memo. The Drill Room generates infinite paper LBOs.
Buy-side · public markets

Hedge fund (long/short equity)

Models on your screen
The quarterly earnings model: a driver-built 3-statement exactly like Model Lab Level 2 (units × price, contribution margins, days), tuned to predict the next print vs consensus; plus scenario/SOTP models for the thesis.
Drivers that matter
The 2 or 3 revenue drivers that actually move the stock, incremental margins, guidance mechanics, consensus numbers.
The output that decides
Your EPS/EBITDA vs the street, quarter by quarter, and what multiple re-rating your variant view implies.
How it is tested
The timed model test (the Level 2 architecture) plus a stock pitch with a variant view.
Advisory · private capital

Private Capital Advisory (PCA) · LP-led secondaries

Models on your screen
The portfolio pricing model: bottoms-up NAV of every fund interest in a portfolio (each company rolled forward from the last mark), discount/premium to NAV, and the buyer's return model at each price.
Drivers that matter
NAV vintage and staleness, unfunded commitments, distribution pacing assumptions, discount rate to the mark, leverage on the deal (deferred payments).
The output that decides
Price as a % of NAV that clears both sides: the seller's need for liquidity vs the buyer's target IRR (typically mid-teens on diversified LP books).
How it is tested
Price this LP position: roll the NAV, assume a cash-flow pacing curve, solve for the discount that hits the return.
Advisory · private capital

GP-led secondaries · continuation vehicles

Models on your screen
The continuation vehicle (CV) model: a single-asset (or strip) LBO-style model where existing LPs choose cash or roll, a new lead investor prices the asset, and the GP resets carry: economics for 3 parties in one build.
Drivers that matter
The asset's forward plan (it is an LBO underwrite), price vs fund NAV (the fairness fight), roll vs sell election rates, new-money need, GP carry crystallization and reset terms.
The output that decides
Returns to the new investor AND fairness to selling LPs: the model must show both, because a conflicted GP sits in the middle.
How it is tested
Underwrite the asset like a buyout, then layer the CV mechanics: who gets what at 2x? Status-quo fund vs CV outcomes.
Buy-side · fund economics

GP stakes

Models on your screen
The management-company model: you are valuing the GP itself: fee-related earnings (fees on committed/invested capital minus comp and opex), carry by fund vintage (probability-weighted, back-loaded), and balance-sheet co-invests: a SOTP where each stream gets its own multiple or DCF.
Drivers that matter
Fundraising trajectory (the real growth driver), fee rates and step-downs, FRE margin, carry realization timing, key-person risk.
The output that decides
Value per stream: high multiple on durable FRE, discounted carry, book value on the balance sheet: and the check that distributions can fund the GP's own commitments.
How it is tested
Value this $10B-AUM manager: separate FRE from carry, defend both multiples. The asset-management industry profile carries the public-market version.
Buy-side · credit

Private credit / direct lending

Models on your screen
The lender's LBO: the same buyout model, graded on downside: base/downside/severe cases, covenant headroom model, recovery analysis, and unitranche return builds (coupon + OID + fees, levered at the fund).
Drivers that matter
Fixed-charge coverage through the downside case, enterprise value cushion beneath the debt, docs (leakage, EBITDA add-backs), pricing (SOFR + spread + OID).
The output that decides
Yield-to-worst vs loss-given-default: does the spread pay for the downside the model shows?
How it is tested
Same LBO, opposite chair: they hand you a sponsor's model and ask what breaks first and at what EBITDA decline you are impaired.
Sell-side · financing

Leveraged finance / DCM

Models on your screen
Debt capacity and pro forma models: leverage through each tranche, pricing grids, covenant models, refinancing economics (call premiums, make-wholes), and rating-agency style metrics builds.
Drivers that matter
Leverage vs the comp set, coverage, market clearing spreads, call schedules, docs flexibility the sponsor wants.
The output that decides
The financing structure that clears the market at the lowest all-in cost while surviving the rating and the downside case.
How it is tested
Credit metrics fluency and live market levels: closest sell-side cousin to RX and private-credit prep.
Sell-side · equity

ECM

Models on your screen
IPO valuation and dilution models (pre/post-money, greenshoe mechanics), follow-on and block pricing models, and converts math (conversion premium, bond floor, hedging).
Drivers that matter
Comparable trading multiples at pricing, the discount to clear size, float and lockups, market windows.
The output that decides
Price and size that clears the book with a healthy aftermarket: judged in public, the day after.
How it is tested
Process fluency plus market feel: recent pricings vs ranges, what the calendar looks like.
Buy-side · early stage

Venture & growth equity

Models on your screen
Cap tables (dilution across rounds, liquidation preferences, option pools), unit-economics models (CAC payback, cohort retention, burn multiple), and for growth: a revenue-build to profitability with the retention stack at its heart.
Drivers that matter
Gross and net revenue retention, growth efficiency, gross margin, dilution path to exit.
The output that decides
Ownership at exit × exit value vs the fund's power-law math: does this deal return the fund?
How it is tested
Cohort table reads, cap-table math (who gets what at a $400M sale with a 1x pref), market-sizing sanity.
Buy-side · real assets

Real estate & infrastructure / project finance

Models on your screen
Property-level models (rent roll to NOI to levered cash flows, cap rates in and out) and project finance models: contracted cash flows, DSCR-sized debt sculpting, 25-year horizons where the debt schedule IS the model.
Drivers that matter
Occupancy/utilization, rent or tariff escalators, cap rate / discount rate, DSCR covenants, refinancing points.
The output that decides
Levered IRR to equity with the debt sized by the coverage ratios, not by wish.
How it is tested
NOI math, cap-rate intuition (what a 5% to 6% move does to value), DSCR mechanics.

The pattern worth noticing. Almost everything above is one of 3 machines wearing different clothes: the 3-statement driver model (earnings, credit, and every operating build: Model Lab Level 2), the leveraged returns model (LBO, CV, lender's LBO, project finance: the Drill Room's paper LBO is its skeleton), and the claims-splitting model (waterfalls, cap tables, merger consideration: who gets what, in what order). Master the 3 machines and every seat's model is a dialect, not a new language.

M.3 The valuation atlas: every lens, and where to see it used

Multiples and DCFs are the defaults, not the whole toolbox. Each method below links 3 ways: the calculator that runs it, the module that teaches it, and industry dossiers where it is genuinely the right tool (every one of the atlas's 79 profiles carries a valuation kit with when-NOT-to-use rows).

Intrinsic

DCF: unlevered (UFCF)

Project the cash the whole business throws off, discount at WACC. The default intrinsic lens for any cash-generative operator with projectable flows.
Links
Toolkit: quick DCF + WACC · Module 04 · worked examples: Semiconductors, SaaS
Intrinsic

FCFE & dividend discount (DDM)

Discount the cash flows to EQUITY at the cost of equity: the right intrinsic tool when leverage IS the business (banks, insurers) or debt follows a fixed schedule (project finance).
Links
Interview bank: DCF category · worked examples: Banking, Insurance, Utilities
Market

Enterprise multiples: EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, EV/Sales, EV/GP

The comparables workhorses. EV/EBIT where capex intensity differs; EV/Sales and EV/gross-profit where profits have not arrived yet: pick the numerator the business model deserves.
Links
Toolkit: comps pricing + EV bridge · Module 04 · worked examples: Aerospace, Media, AI (EV/revenue)
Market

P/E and PEG

The equity-holder's multiple: right when leverage is comparable and earnings are the product (mature compounders, staples, banks in normal times). PEG adds a growth haircut, roughly.
Links
Model Lab Level 2's valuation strip · worked examples: Payments (the compounder premium), Consumer goods
Market

Precedent transactions

What control actually traded for, premiums included. Evidence of buyer behavior, staler and noisier than trading comps: the M&A negotiation anchor.
Links
Module 05 · Interview bank: valuation · worked example: Investment Banking
Sector-native

P/B against ROE

For balance-sheet businesses: a bank earning above its cost of equity deserves book value or more; below, less. The scatterplot every FIG banker lives on.
Links
worked examples: Banking, Insurance · FIG groups test it
Sector-native

NAV / sum-of-the-assets

Value the assets one by one: oil and gas reserves (PV-10), mining ore bodies, REIT portfolios, closed-end funds. The market's discount or premium to NAV then becomes the debate.
Links
worked examples: Oil & Gas, Mining, Real Estate · the PCA seat prices fund NAVs the same way
Sector-native

Cap rates

Real estate's inverse multiple: NOI over value. A 5% cap is 20x NOI; a move to 6% is a (17)% value hit with no change in the building. Duration risk in property costume.
Links
worked example: Real Estate · Toolkit: comps logic applies directly
Sector-native

EV/ARR, NRR & Rule of 40

Software's dialect: recurring revenue multiples graded by retention quality and the growth-plus-margin sum. Never pay an ARR multiple without reading the retention stack first.
Links
worked examples: SaaS, Cybersecurity · Interview bank: PE category's growth-equity screen
Sector-native

AUM, FRE multiples & regulated asset base (RAB)

Fee businesses price per dollar managed or per dollar of fee-related earnings; regulated utilities price off the asset base regulators let them earn on. The multiple follows the annuity's quality.
Links
worked examples: Asset Management, Private Equity, Utilities · the GP-stakes seat is this, applied
Deal-based

LBO / ability-to-pay (the floor)

What a sponsor could pay and still clear its return hurdle at feasible leverage: a valuation FLOOR under any cash-generative asset, and a bid predictor in every sale process.
Links
Drill Room: paper LBO · Toolkit: LBO returns · Module 06
Deal-based

SOTP, liquidation & replacement cost

Sum-of-the-parts for conglomerates (each segment on its own multiple, minus a conglomerate discount); liquidation value for distress (the recovery floor); replacement cost for heavy assets (why build when you can buy below it).
Links
SOTP: Media · liquidation: the RX worked example · replacement cost: Steel, Shipping

How to choose: match the lens to where the value lives: future cash flows (DCF family), comparable market prices (multiples), the assets themselves (NAV, replacement, liquidation), or what a specific buyer can pay (LBO, precedents). Strong answers name 2 lenses, say which one deserves more weight HERE, and why: the football field in Module 04 is that judgment, drawn.

M.4 The driver atlas: how each industry's model is actually built

A 3-statement model is a template; the revenue build is where the industry lives. Every business reduces to a formula: units times price, assets times yield, users times monetization. Open each cluster for the build, the drivers that move it, and the quirk that breaks generic models. Every row links to the full dossier.

Software & internet recurring + usage
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
SaaS Rev = opening ARR x NRR + new bookingsRecurring base rolled forward, then new logos layered on. Net revenue retention (110 to 130% is elite), gross churn, CAC payback in months of S&M, Rule of 40 (growth % + FCF margin %). Bookings, billings, deferred revenue, and revenue are 4 different lines on 4 different clocks; cash collects ahead of revenue, so working capital is a tailwind. Pre-profit names price on EV/ARR or EV/gross profit.
Marketplaces Rev = GMV x take rate (3P) or GMV at retail margin (1P)GMV itself = active buyers x order frequency x AOV. Buyer growth and frequency, take-rate ceiling (what sellers tolerate), fulfillment cost per order, ad-revenue attach. 1P vs 3P mix moves reported revenue enormously at identical GMV. Model contribution profit per order, not the P&L average.
Cloud & hyperscale Rev = workloads x usage x unit priceCommitted contracts (RPO) convert to recognized revenue on consumption. RPO growth, net expansion within accounts, capex-to-capacity lead times, price cuts passed through as volume. Capex leads revenue by 12 to 24 months: depreciation schedules and server lives (extended twice industry-wide) quietly set the margin trajectory.
AI & compute Rev = capacity online x utilization x $/unit (tokens, GPU-hours) Power secured (the real constraint), chip supply, $/token deflation vs volume growth, enterprise commitments. Unit prices fall 10x while volumes rise faster: never extrapolate either alone. Depreciation life on accelerators is the entire margin debate.
Financials balance sheets & flows
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Banking NII = earning assets x NIM; + fee incomeThe balance sheet is modeled first; the income statement falls out of it. Loan growth, deposit beta (how fast funding reprices), provisions/credit costs, efficiency ratio, CET1 headroom. An unlevered DCF is meaningless: leverage IS the business. Value on P/TBV against ROTE; capital requirements cap growth before demand does.
Insurance UW profit = premiums earned x (1 - combined ratio); + float x yield Rate change vs loss trend, combined ratio by line, reserve development, investment yield on the float. Reserves are estimates that get re-marked for years (development); premiums earn over the policy term, not at sale. P/B vs ROE is the pricing grammar.
Asset management Rev = average AUM x fee rate (bps)AUM rolls: opening + net flows + market performance. Net flows (the only part management controls), fee compression, mix shift to passive or alts, performance fees. Incremental margins on AUM run 60 to 70%: operating leverage cuts both ways. For alternatives, value fee-related earnings and carry separately (the GP-stakes math on the PCA desk).
Payments Rev = TPV x take rate, net of incentives Volume growth (cash displacement), cross-border mix (5 to 10x the domestic yield), incentive contra-revenue, value-added services attach. Gross vs net revenue presentation differs by player: compare take rates on the same basis or not at all.
Exchanges Rev = ADV x capture rate + recurring data/index fees Volatility (drives volumes), capture per contract, data and index subscription growth. Transaction revenue is a volatility derivative; data revenue is SaaS. Sum-of-the-parts the 2 or misprice both.
Healthcare patients x price x policy
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Pharma Rev = sum of franchises: patients x net price x penetrationEach asset falls to 10 to 20% of peak on patent expiry. Exclusivity dates, pipeline probability-of-success, gross-to-net spread, launch curves. Model per asset, not in aggregate: a company DCF is a sum of drug DCFs plus a pipeline of options. The cliff is a schedule, not a risk.
Medical devices Rev = installed base x utilization x consumable pull-through + new placements Procedure volumes, razor-to-razorblade attach rates, new-product cycles, reimbursement decisions. The recurring consumables mix decides the multiple; placements sold at a loss to seed the annuity are a feature, not a bug.
Hospitals & providers Rev = volumes (admissions, visits) x revenue per caseRevenue per case = acuity mix x payor mix rates. Volumes and acuity, payor mix (commercial subsidizes government), labor cost inflation vs rate escalators. You do not set half your prices: Medicare and Medicaid rates arrive by rulemaking. Labor is 50%+ of cost and the whole model swing.
Payors Rev = members x premium PMPM; margin = 1 - MLR - SG&A Membership by segment, medical loss ratio (~85% regulated floor), utilization trend vs pricing cycle. 100 bps of MLR is roughly the entire net margin move: the model is a loss-ratio forecast wearing a revenue line.
Consumer volume + price/mix
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Retail Rev = stores x sales per store; growth = comps + net new units Same-store sales split into traffic vs ticket, gross margin (markdowns, shrink), inventory turns, e-commerce mix. New-unit math is its own model: 4-wall EBITDA, cash-on-cash payback, cannibalization. A retailer growing units with negative comps is renting growth.
Consumer goods Organic growth = volume + price/mixFX and M&A stripped out; the bridge every quarter reports. Category growth, share, price elasticity, input-cost pass-through, A&P reinvestment rate. Price-led growth with falling volumes is borrowed time: the volume line is the health of the franchise; the price line is the health of the brand.
Restaurants Rev = units x AUV; franchised units pay a ~5% royalty instead Comps, unit growth, franchise mix, food and labor inflation vs menu pricing. Franchised and company-owned are different businesses in one ticker: a royalty stream (high multiple) vs restaurant margins (low one). Refranchising changes optics, not cash.
Luxury Rev = volume x price, with volume deliberately constrained Pricing power (the annual raise), the China cluster, DTC vs wholesale mix, brand heat. Scarcity is the model: pushing volume to make a quarter is how a luxury brand becomes a premium brand. Watch wholesale channel stuffing as the first crack.
Industrials & transport capacity x utilization x yield
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Airlines Rev = ASMs x load factor x yield (= RASM x ASMs) Capacity discipline (industry ASM growth vs GDP), fuel and hedges, CASM ex-fuel, ancillary attach. Fixed costs mean 1 point of load factor or yield swings EBIT violently. Fuel passes through only when the whole industry stays disciplined, which it periodically does not.
Aerospace Rev = deliveries x price + aftermarket (installed base x flight hours) Build rates, backlog in years, aftermarket margins (25 to 30%) vs OE (near zero), shop-visit cycles. OE loses money to build the installed base; the aftermarket annuity is the business. Model the fleet like an insurance float.
Freight & logistics Rev = volume (loads, TEUs, parcels) x yield per unit Tonnage vs pricing cycle (they alternate), network density, fuel surcharges, contract vs spot mix. Density is margin: the incremental package on an existing route is nearly free. Rails and trucking speak in operating ratio (OR), not EBITDA margin.
Autos Rev = units x ASP; captive finance modeled separately Mix (trucks, EVs), incentives per unit, plant utilization (breakeven ~80%), raw materials. Consolidate industrial and finco and you value neither: split them. Production swings working capital hard in both directions.
Energy, materials & real assets price takers, asset models
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Upstream oil & gas Rev = production x realized price (after hedges and differentials) Decline rates (shale wells fall 30 to 40% in year 1), reinvestment rate, breakevens, hedge book. The treadmill: flat production requires constant drilling. Model at the well level and value on NAV (PV-10); multiples on peak-cycle earnings are how buyers overpay.
Utilities EPS = rate base x allowed ROE x equity share Rate-base growth (approved capex), allowed ROE by jurisdiction, regulatory lag, load growth (data centers changed this line). Capex IS the growth algorithm: the model is a regulatory filing. Payout ratios and equity issuance close the loop; the risk is a commission, not a competitor.
Mining Rev = tonnes x grade x recovery x price Ore grade (declining almost everywhere), cash-cost curve position, sustaining capex, permitting timelines. Price is exogenous: model the cost curve position and let price scenarios do the rest. NAV at long-run prices beats spot multiples.
Chemicals Rev = volume x price; profit = spread over feedstock Industry utilization (above ~90%, pricing flies), feedstock spreads (ethane vs naphtha), capacity additions in China. Commodity and specialty deserve different multiples inside the same company. The real model is the spread, not the revenue line.
Real estate NOI = GLA x rent x occupancy - opex; value = NOI / cap rate Occupancy, releasing spreads, escalators, development yield vs cost of capital. REITs speak AFFO per share, not EPS (depreciation is non-economic here). Every valuation is a cap-rate argument in disguise.
TMT infrastructure & media subs, cycles, capacity
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Telecom Rev = subscribers x ARPU, by segment Net adds, churn, ARPU accretion from bundles, capex at 15 to 20% of revenue, spectrum outlays. EBITDA flatters: EBITDA less capex is the real currency. Leases and spectrum obligations belong in the EV bridge or the comps lie.
Media & streaming Rev = subs x ARPU + advertising (impressions x CPM) Net adds and churn, ad-tier mix, content spend, engagement per sub. Content amortization policy is the margin: cash content spend and P&L amortization diverge for years. The gap is the model.
Semiconductors Rev = units x ASP, through a cycle Design wins (locked years early), fab utilization, inventory in the channel, capex cycles. Model the cycle, not the year: normalize margins to mid-cycle before applying a multiple, or buy the top with confidence.
Data centers Rev = capacity leased (MW) x rate x occupancy Bookings vs capacity online, escalators, development yield on cost vs WACC, power procurement. The constraint moved from demand to electrons: signed-but-not-commissioned backlog is the growth line. AFFO and cap-rate grammar, like REITs.
Services the user's example: unit x volume
IndustryThe revenue buildThe driversThe quirk to respect
Professional services Rev = billable headcount x utilization x realized rateInvestment banking is the atlas's worked example of people-leverage economics. Utilization (70 to 80% target), rate escalation vs realization (discounts), attrition, the pyramid's leverage ratio. The pyramid is the margin: partners sell, associates produce, and the ratio between them is the business model. The assets ride the elevator down every night.
Route-based services Rev = units served x volume x price per unitWaste, distribution, uniforms, pest control: the route is the asset. Route density, contract escalators (CPI-linked), retention, tuck-in M&A at 5 to 7x synergized. Density economics: the incremental stop on an existing route is nearly all margin, which is why these industries consolidate forever and the multiples never look cheap.
Education Rev = enrollment x tuition (net of discounting) Enrollment funnels, discount rates (the sticker is fiction), regulatory funding per student, online mix. Demographics are the demand curve and they are published 18 years in advance: the rare industry where the volume forecast is nearly certain and still ignored.
