M.2 The models, seat by seat

Sell-side · advisory

M&A group

Models on your screen
Merger model (accretion/dilution with PPA and synergies), DCF, trading comps and precedents, ability-to-pay LBO (what a sponsor could bid), sum-of-the-parts, and the football field that stitches them together.
Drivers that matter
Price and premium, financing mix, synergies net of costs-to-achieve, purchase accounting (intangible amortization), pro forma leverage.
The output that decides
The accretion/dilution table across financing scenarios, and where the offer lands on the football field.
How it is tested
Merger math aloud, then judgment: is accretive good? Where did the synergies go? Module 05 + the Drill Room's snap calls.
Sell-side · advisory

Restructuring (RX)

Models on your screen
Capital structure waterfall (recovery by class at a range of EVs), 13-week cash flow, liquidity runway model, DIP sizing, and the reorganized-company business plan model that valuation fights are fought over.
Drivers that matter
Enterprise value (the whole fight), class-by-class claims and liens, cash burn by week, DIP terms, plan treatment.
The output that decides
Where value breaks: the fulcrum security, and each class's recovery per point of EV.
How it is tested
Build a waterfall from a cap table, find the fulcrum, defend the EV. The interview bank's RX category is the verbal version.
Buy-side · control

Private equity (buyout)

Models on your screen
The LBO model in every grade: paper, 3-hour test, full operating model with debt schedule and cash sweep; plus add-on models, covenant models, and the monthly portfolio-company reporting build.
Drivers that matter
Entry/exit multiple, leverage and its cost, EBITDA growth by lever (price, volume, cost, add-ons), FCF conversion, fees.
The output that decides
MOIC and IRR with attribution: how much came from operations vs leverage vs multiple.
How it is tested
Paper LBO aloud, timed model test, then the case-study memo. The Drill Room generates infinite paper LBOs.
Buy-side · public markets

Hedge fund (long/short equity)

Models on your screen
The quarterly earnings model: a driver-built 3-statement exactly like Model Lab Level 2 (units × price, contribution margins, days), tuned to predict the next print vs consensus; plus scenario/SOTP models for the thesis.
Drivers that matter
The 2 or 3 revenue drivers that actually move the stock, incremental margins, guidance mechanics, consensus numbers.
The output that decides
Your EPS/EBITDA vs the street, quarter by quarter, and what multiple re-rating your variant view implies.
How it is tested
The timed model test (the Level 2 architecture) plus a stock pitch with a variant view.
Advisory · private capital

Private Capital Advisory (PCA) · LP-led secondaries

Models on your screen
The portfolio pricing model: bottoms-up NAV of every fund interest in a portfolio (each company rolled forward from the last mark), discount/premium to NAV, and the buyer's return model at each price.
Drivers that matter
NAV vintage and staleness, unfunded commitments, distribution pacing assumptions, discount rate to the mark, leverage on the deal (deferred payments).
The output that decides
Price as a % of NAV that clears both sides: the seller's need for liquidity vs the buyer's target IRR (typically mid-teens on diversified LP books).
How it is tested
Price this LP position: roll the NAV, assume a cash-flow pacing curve, solve for the discount that hits the return.
Advisory · private capital

GP-led secondaries · continuation vehicles

Models on your screen
The continuation vehicle (CV) model: a single-asset (or strip) LBO-style model where existing LPs choose cash or roll, a new lead investor prices the asset, and the GP resets carry: economics for 3 parties in one build.
Drivers that matter
The asset's forward plan (it is an LBO underwrite), price vs fund NAV (the fairness fight), roll vs sell election rates, new-money need, GP carry crystallization and reset terms.
The output that decides
Returns to the new investor AND fairness to selling LPs: the model must show both, because a conflicted GP sits in the middle.
How it is tested
Underwrite the asset like a buyout, then layer the CV mechanics: who gets what at 2x? Status-quo fund vs CV outcomes.
Buy-side · fund economics

GP stakes

Models on your screen
The management-company model: you are valuing the GP itself: fee-related earnings (fees on committed/invested capital minus comp and opex), carry by fund vintage (probability-weighted, back-loaded), and balance-sheet co-invests: a SOTP where each stream gets its own multiple or DCF.
Drivers that matter
Fundraising trajectory (the real growth driver), fee rates and step-downs, FRE margin, carry realization timing, key-person risk.
The output that decides
Value per stream: high multiple on durable FRE, discounted carry, book value on the balance sheet: and the check that distributions can fund the GP's own commitments.
How it is tested
Value this $10B-AUM manager: separate FRE from carry, defend both multiples. The asset-management industry profile carries the public-market version.
Buy-side · credit

Private credit / direct lending

Models on your screen
The lender's LBO: the same buyout model, graded on downside: base/downside/severe cases, covenant headroom model, recovery analysis, and unitranche return builds (coupon + OID + fees, levered at the fund).
Drivers that matter
Fixed-charge coverage through the downside case, enterprise value cushion beneath the debt, docs (leakage, EBITDA add-backs), pricing (SOFR + spread + OID).
The output that decides
Yield-to-worst vs loss-given-default: does the spread pay for the downside the model shows?
How it is tested
Same LBO, opposite chair: they hand you a sponsor's model and ask what breaks first and at what EBITDA decline you are impaired.
Sell-side · financing

Leveraged finance / DCM

Models on your screen
Debt capacity and pro forma models: leverage through each tranche, pricing grids, covenant models, refinancing economics (call premiums, make-wholes), and rating-agency style metrics builds.
Drivers that matter
Leverage vs the comp set, coverage, market clearing spreads, call schedules, docs flexibility the sponsor wants.
The output that decides
The financing structure that clears the market at the lowest all-in cost while surviving the rating and the downside case.
How it is tested
Credit metrics fluency and live market levels: closest sell-side cousin to RX and private-credit prep.
Sell-side · equity

ECM

Models on your screen
IPO valuation and dilution models (pre/post-money, greenshoe mechanics), follow-on and block pricing models, and converts math (conversion premium, bond floor, hedging).
Drivers that matter
Comparable trading multiples at pricing, the discount to clear size, float and lockups, market windows.
The output that decides
Price and size that clears the book with a healthy aftermarket: judged in public, the day after.
How it is tested
Process fluency plus market feel: recent pricings vs ranges, what the calendar looks like.
Buy-side · early stage

Venture & growth equity

Models on your screen
Cap tables (dilution across rounds, liquidation preferences, option pools), unit-economics models (CAC payback, cohort retention, burn multiple), and for growth: a revenue-build to profitability with the retention stack at its heart.
Drivers that matter
Gross and net revenue retention, growth efficiency, gross margin, dilution path to exit.
The output that decides
Ownership at exit × exit value vs the fund's power-law math: does this deal return the fund?
How it is tested
Cohort table reads, cap-table math (who gets what at a $400M sale with a 1x pref), market-sizing sanity.
Buy-side · real assets

Real estate & infrastructure / project finance

Models on your screen
Property-level models (rent roll to NOI to levered cash flows, cap rates in and out) and project finance models: contracted cash flows, DSCR-sized debt sculpting, 25-year horizons where the debt schedule IS the model.
Drivers that matter
Occupancy/utilization, rent or tariff escalators, cap rate / discount rate, DSCR covenants, refinancing points.
The output that decides
Levered IRR to equity with the debt sized by the coverage ratios, not by wish.
How it is tested
NOI math, cap-rate intuition (what a 5% to 6% move does to value), DSCR mechanics.

The pattern worth noticing. Almost everything above is one of 3 machines wearing different clothes: the 3-statement driver model (earnings, credit, and every operating build: Model Lab Level 2), the leveraged returns model (LBO, CV, lender's LBO, project finance: the Drill Room's paper LBO is its skeleton), and the claims-splitting model (waterfalls, cap tables, merger consideration: who gets what, in what order). Master the 3 machines and every seat's model is a dialect, not a new language.
