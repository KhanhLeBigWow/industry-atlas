P.1 The primer: why this market exists

Private equity funds lock capital for 10+ years, but nobody's life waits 10 years. Secondaries are the release valve: a market where fund stakes and even whole portfolio companies change hands mid-life, at a price negotiated against NAV.

The market in numbers: secondaries volume set a record around $160B in 2024 and kept climbing in 2025 (Jefferies/Evercore market reviews track it half-yearly, free), split roughly half LP-led (institutions selling portfolios of fund stakes) and half GP-led (sponsors moving prize assets into continuation vehicles). The engine behind the growth: distributions from regular exits have run below 15% of NAV for years, so sellers manufacture liquidity instead of waiting for it. LP portfolios of buyout funds have cleared in the high-80s to mid-90s % of NAV in recent years; venture books materially lower: the discount IS the market's honest opinion of the marks.

The 3 businesses inside a PCA desk: (1) LP advisory: run a competitive process to sell an institution's fund portfolio: price discovery against stale NAVs, transfer mechanics, GP consents. (2) GP-led advisory: design and place continuation vehicles: pick the asset, set the price with a fairness process, find a lead buyer, run the LP election. (3) Primary placement: raise new funds for GPs (the classic Park Hill / placement-agent business), plus co-investment syndication alongside. One desk, three fee streams, all feeding on the same relationships.

P.2 The technicals: the math the desk runs on

BuildThe mechanicsWhat decides the answer
LP portfolio pricingRoll every fund's NAV forward from the last mark (calls, distributions, estimated appreciation), project each fund's remaining cash flows on a pacing curve, discount at the buyer's target return, sum to a price expressed as % of NAV.Mark staleness (a June NAV priced in December), unfunded commitments (a liability the buyer assumes), and the pacing assumption: earlier distributions justify smaller discounts.
Continuation vehicle (CV)A single-asset LBO underwrite wearing fund clothing: forward plan for the asset, entry price vs fund NAV (the fairness fight), new capital need, then the waterfall: rolling LPs, cash-electing LPs, the new lead, and the GP's crystallized-then-reset carry.The lead buyer's underwrite (it IS a buyout), the price-vs-NAV optics, election rates, and whether the GP rolls its crystallized carry (the alignment tell).
Deferred structuresBuyers pay in tranches (e.g. 50% now, 50% in 18 months): effectively leverage that lifts the headline % of NAV without lifting the economics.Always restate a deferred price to present value before comparing bids: a 97% of NAV bid half-deferred can be worth less than 93% cash.
Co-investment returnsDirect equity in a single deal alongside the sponsor, typically fee-free and carry-free: the same LBO model, minus the 2-and-20 drag.Selection risk replaces fee drag: co-invest programs live or die on which deals sponsors actually show them, and how fast they can commit (2 to 4 weeks).
GP-stakes valuationValue the management company itself: fee-related earnings on a durable multiple, carry by vintage probability-weighted and discounted, balance-sheet co-invests at book: a 3-stream SOTP.Fundraising trajectory above all: the next flagship's size moves FRE more than anything; then FRE margin durability and key-person risk.

Quick math to carry into interviews: a buyer targeting a 1.6x on an LP book that returns capital over ~5 years is pricing roughly a mid-teens IRR; every 5 points of NAV discount buys roughly 1 to 1.5 points of IRR on typical pacing. Deferred payment of half the price for 18 months adds ~3 to 4 points of IRR at today's rates: which is exactly why deferrals became standard.

P.6 The study kit: 2 weeks to conversational, 6 to dangerous

Weeks 1-2: fund mechanics cold (waterfalls, GP/LP economics: Module 06), the pricing logic in P.2 said aloud, and the latest Jefferies/Evercore half-year secondaries review read twice (both free). Weeks 3-4: build a toy LP-pricing model (5 funds, pacing curve, solve price for a 1.6x) and a toy CV waterfall (roll vs cash vs new money at 2.0x and 3.0x outcomes). Weeks 5-6: the live market: know 2 recent large CVs and 1 large LP portfolio sale from the trade press, the current % of NAV clearing levels by strategy, and have a view on the semi-liquid boom. Then take the PE category questions and answer them from the SECONDARIES chair: the cross-angle is exactly what these desks test.
