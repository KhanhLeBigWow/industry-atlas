D.1 The primer

M&A bankers run processes and price control. Sell-sides (auctions or negotiated sales), buy-sides (advising an acquirer), defense (activists and hostiles), and fairness opinions: the fee is a % of deal value, and the product is a market cleared in your client's favor.

The rhythm of the desk: a sell-side runs ~4 to 6 months: materials (CIM, model), outreach, first bids, management meetings, final bids against a draft contract, signing, closing. The analyst's life is the model, the materials, and the data room; the associate runs the checklist; the MD runs the price talk. Deal flow follows CEO confidence, financing cost, and sponsor dry powder: the cycle every coverage group rides. Sponsors have grown to roughly a third of activity, which is why the financing desks and M&A live in each other's models.

D.4 The landscape

League tables are the desk's scoreboard: Goldman and Morgan Stanley trade the top; JPMorgan brings the balance sheet; the elite boutiques (Evercore, Centerview, Lazard, PJT, Moelis) keep taking advisory share because conflict-free advice sells at mega-cap boards. Across the table sits corporate development (the in-house buyers), sponsors (a third of the market), and the lawyers whose markups set half the real negotiation. The Firms & Groups page maps who is strong where; sector depth lives in the industry groups and their dossiers.

D.5 The study kit

Merger math cold (the drill until instant), Module 05 end to end, 3 live deals you can discuss with a view (premium, logic, market reaction), and 1 broken deal and why. The M&A interview is the most standardized on the street: the differentiation is judgment on deals, not formula recall.
